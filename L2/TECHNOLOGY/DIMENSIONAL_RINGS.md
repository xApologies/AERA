# Dimensional Rings — recursive-topology conservation canon
**Source authority:** `provenance/technology-v0.6.0/sources/DIMENSIONAL_RING_MASTER.pdf` (20 pages), marked **LOCKED-IN SERIES VERSION v1.0**. The exact MK147 device model is preserved here as an available AERA implementation, not a compulsory object for all authors.

## Identity and negative constraints
A dimensional ring is **not** a pocket dimension, infinite inventory, menu, operating system, database, or object list. It is a **recursive-topology conservation device** using configuration space, conscious selection, collapse resolution, inheritance reassignment, recursive persistence and information conservation.

The source states: **the object never ceases to exist; its inheritance changes**. The ring does not contain a physical storage volume. It maintains recursively stabilized topology and conserves identity, structure, topology and relationships. Do not replace the mechanism with teleportation through an invented spatial pocket.

## High-level mechanism
`Genesis energy → expand configuration space → conscious selection of topology → collapse/reassign inheritance → recursively maintain persistence → conserve information.`

### Storage
`Expand → Select → Collapse → Conserve`

1. Activate ring and supply Genesis energy.
2. Expand accessible configuration space into the environment.
3. Consciously select an object within that domain.
4. Collapse resolves the object's inheritance from its original environment to the ring.
5. Recursive topology maintains the object's persistence.

Source example: `Room → Pillow` becomes `Ring → Pillow`. The pillow persists throughout.

### Retrieval
`Recognize → Select → Collapse → Retrieve`

1. Recognize the stored object's topological identity consciously.
2. Select it and resolve collapse.
3. Reassign inheritance from the ring to the receiving environment.
4. The object becomes physically accessible again.

Source example: `Ring → Pillow` becomes `Environment → Pillow`. **No traditional object movement** is required by this account; inheritance changes.

## Configuration-space reach and quality
Energy supplied controls the accessible configuration radius. More energy permits access to a larger portion of the surrounding environment. Source examples contrast objects at 3 feet, 20 feet and 30+ feet, with increasing energy demand.

## Why the wearable ring form
Always worn, accessible, difficult to lose, lightweight, durable, hands-free, expedition-friendly. The source allows cubes, necklaces, bracelets and talismans; **rings are the civilization's practical standard**, not a mathematical necessity.

## Conscious recognition rather than inventory UI
No inventory menu, object list, search field or automatic item index. Stored objects acquire recognizable topological identities; the user becomes familiar with them over time. Retrieval resembles feeling for keys in a pocket.

A novice may be slow and inconsistent; an expert may retrieve almost immediately. **The ring did not improve; the user improved.** Familiarity and organization matter.

## Jenkins principle — canonical narrative example
Jenkins stores rope, lantern, shovel, fishing pole, cooking pot, bridge sections and assorted junk. At a 300-foot chasm he tries to retrieve rope, but first produces lanterns, pots, shovels, fishing gear and other clutter. Eventually he finds rope. The ring functioned correctly; the user's disorganization created the delay. This illustrates a narrative limitation without an inventory accounting minigame.

## Narrative and civilization consequences
Dimensional rings remove tedious pack-weight and inventory bookkeeping while retaining meaningful route, supply, obstacle and expedition decisions. They are foundational expedition infrastructure **in the MK147 setting**, comparable to maps, lanterns, climbing equipment and Genesis crystals. Professional delvers are expected to own one; better rings are major investments. This is a source-world norm, not a universal AERA profession rule.

Different levels of understanding coexist: a delver says “storage ring”; a scholar says “configuration-space storage”; an Architect says “conserved recursive topology through configuration-space expansion and inheritance reassignment.”
