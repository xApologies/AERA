# eldris — persistent recursive collapse

**Primary authority:** the author's uploaded *MK147 MASTER MODEL — ELDRIS / LRS*, sections 0–18. The original uses **ELDRIS**; active prose uses `eldris`. Dictation variants such as “Elgis” do not name a different entity. **LRS**, where used by this source, means a localized recursive structure produced by Genesis/chirality collapse; it refers to the same concept in this context.

> **The collapse did not stop. It stabilized.**

## 1. What the entity is

An eldris is a bounded process that persists as an entity. Local Genesis instability undergoes runaway recursive chirality propagation and reaches basin-dominant closure rather than dispersing back into the field. The ongoing collapse stabilizes; it does not simply cease and leave an ordinary finished object. The master calls this **Persistent Recursive Topology** generated by runaway Genesis collapse and stabilized through chirality closure. [Master §§0–2, pp. 1–2.]

Its structural admissibility is described in terms of persistence, stability and closure, not good or evil. Civilization calls it a monster because of its behavior and effects. It is not a demon, ordinary animal, conventional elemental creature, or a body whose whole surface is painted its basin color.

The master contains dungeon-born framing in §§0 and 11. AERA's existing generalization permits formation wherever suitable local collapse stabilizes, including storm environments. The original wording remains in the source; the scope comparison is explicit in [Source Boundaries](../../provenance/environment-v0.5.0/SOURCE_BOUNDARIES.md). This is not an unrecorded edit to the supplied authority.

## 2. Formation sequence

The source's basic sequence is:

```text
Local Genesis instability
  → runaway recursive chirality propagation
  → energy-density basin composition destabilizes
  → one basin becomes dominant
  → projection / closure bounds the runaway
  → persistent recursive topology stabilizes
  → eldris
```

Its expanded sequence follows a field disturbance into recursive collapse, propagation through chirality structure, a resolution attempt, folding back into itself, basin-dominant closure and a bounded monster-structure. These descriptions preserve the master; they do not add a new numerical closure operator. [Master §2, p. 2.]

**The basin is not the monster. The collapse by itself is not the monster. The eldris is the closure-state of the collapse.**

## 3. Body, traces and identity

An eldris typically appears black, wispy, rootlike, smoky, branching, fractured or shadow-structured. The collapse contains the basin expression so tightly that color is visible mainly through limited leakage: eyes, claws, wounds, inner glow, breath, impact effects, branching traces and behavior. A Red eldris is not a uniformly red animal; Red identifies the basin whose identity dominates its closure. [Master §§3–4, pp. 2–3.]

The source distinguishes two aspects:

| Collapse-body aspect | Basin-identity aspect |
|---|---|
| Black appearance and recursive body-form. | Behavior and interaction profile. |
| Wispy or unstable edges. | Energy-density dominance and combat expression. |
| Containment and persistence of the process. | The chromatic family used in encounter classification. |

The source shorthand is **eldris type = collapse body + basin identity**. It is a descriptive distinction, not a newly derived quantitative equation.

The black collapse signature is separate from Kira's Black-spectrum identity in MK231. Shared visual blackness does not merge their origins.

## 4. Physical substance

The master explains substance through **energy-mass closure**. Eldris can claw, strike, block a path, receive a hit, and be killed or dispersed. Their substance is not ordinary flesh: it is basin-structured collapse with sufficient persistent topology to interact physically. They are not an illusion or a merely visual projection. [Master §10, p. 8.]

The source's story-language formulation describes collapsed Genesis energy persisting as a body. It does not supply a universal biological anatomy, damage equation, material density or numerical dispersal threshold.

## 5. Six basin-dominant families

The following are the master's inherited behavior families. They do not assign the six chromatic energy basins one-to-one to the six operational Transduction domains. [Master §§5–6, pp. 4–7.]

### Red — physical contact · Category A

Red expresses persistence and physicality: claws, teeth, melee and body-force. It is the most basic stable monster expression in the source, but not harmless. Contact can kill. Its black body may reveal red eye-traces or other internal marks. The “fuzzy ball” description conveys low coherence and density relative to the later families, not a requirement that all Red eldris be spherical.

### Orange — intrusion · Category A

Orange adds basin-energy intrusion to physical contact. A claw strike may inject energy into a target rather than merely cut it. Source-described consequences include fatigue, destabilization, internal damage and increased healing-load pressure. The familiar need to avoid contact remains, but the consequences of contact differ from Red.

### Yellow — force-bearing projection · Category A

Yellow introduces ranged energy-mass projection: orbs, beams, rings and projected force. These attacks are not simply visible light. The source gives examples of an orb knocking someone down, a beam denting armor and a ring acting like an expanding wall. Spacing, cover, shields, barriers and line-of-sight discipline therefore matter. This is a tactical change within Category A, not a fixed range or damage allocation.

### Green — reinforced persistence · Category B

Green is the source's first major threat jump: resonance and structural reinforcement produce juggernaut-like persistence. It is not just Yellow plus additional abilities. It can be larger and harder to disperse because its reinforced organization resists collapse.

The master illustrates this with a nine-foot juggernaut and a group potentially taking an hour to defeat one. Those are examples, not universal size or kill-time rules. The practical point is the professional burden of sustained attention, endurance and formation while a resistant threat remains active.

### Blue — expressed projection · Category B

Blue develops the projected-force family into effects that act on and after impact. The master contrasts Yellow force with Blue expression: a Yellow orb knocks back, whereas a Blue orb can explode; a Yellow beam pushes against armor, whereas a Blue beam can burn through it. Rings may detonate, shear, burn or restructure the space they cross. The list describes the source's range of expressions, not a declaration that every Blue eldris has every listed attack.

This distinction must survive a summary: **Yellow force hits; Blue expression acts.** Formation discipline is consequently important in the source's encounter language.

### Violet — high Genesis expression · Category C

Violet is not merely stronger Blue. It behaves as a high Genesis-expression entity, with maege-like manifestations that the source's inhabitants poorly understand. Detailed knowledge is rare; it is presented as a legendary threat, not an invitation to invent an exhaustive spell catalogue in this pass.

## 6. Threat scale versus family identity

| Broad category | Included families | Source interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| A | Red, Orange, Yellow | Distinct contact, intrusion and ranged-force profiles within the lower broad regime. |
| B | Green, Blue | Professional threshold: reinforced persistence and advanced expressed projection. |
| C | Violet | Separate legendary regime with limited ordinary knowledge. |

The master also gives transition shorthand: Red→Orange one jump; Orange→Yellow one; Yellow→Green two; Green→Blue two; Blue→Violet three. Preserve it as a **qualitative threat comparison**, not a linear damage or energy multiplier:

```text
R -- O -- Y ==== G ==== B ====== V
```

**Supplemental inherited palette:** the audited MK157 general eldris reference explicitly has **no White eldris**. The supplied master enumerates Red–Violet but does not state that negative rule in those exact words. This distinction is recorded rather than attributing the supplemental rule to the PDF. A White domai core is not a White eldris. [Source register: MK157 palette excerpt.]

## 7. Chirality, Genesis and closed-manifold intuition

The master's chirality description connects propagation, boundary, persistence, closure, admissibility and recursive inheritance. Its operational summary is: chirality propagates; collapse localizes; closure stabilizes; persistence admits the entity. [Master §7, p. 7.]

The source then compares normal matter-like stabilization with eldris: both are Genesis structures, but normal organization stabilizes into matter-like structure whereas the eldris stabilizes a runaway recursive-collapse process into monster-like structure. The difference lies in closure behavior. [Master §8, pp. 7–8.]

Section 9 uses **S3 / closed-manifold intuition**: runaway propagation becomes bounded through projection and closure rather than expanding indefinitely. The source itself calls this structural intuition, not literal final mathematics. Preserve that framing; this pass does not replace it with a new theorem or operator.

## 8. Appearance and recognition in a scene

The visual source permits distorted antlers, horns and limbs, smoky edges, rootlike branching, an unstable-looking silhouette and limited glowing traces. The body may look half-formed while remaining physically persistent. It is not automatically undead, demonic, elemental or an ordinary animal. [Master §13, p. 10.]

Delvers identify family from **behavior and trace**, not full-body pigment. They observe whether it claws, intrudes, projects force, persists as a resonant juggernaut, produces secondary energy effects, or manifests high Genesis effects. First contact matters because an unidentified black shape has not yet disclosed the relevant threat profile. [Master §14, pp. 10–11.]

## 9. Dungeons and adjacent source concepts

Eldris are one possible output of a dungeon's oscillating admissible states, alongside noncombat outputs. The complete source connection is retained in [Dungeons](03_DUNGEONS.md). Dungeon containment does not restrict all eldris origin to dungeons.

The master also compares identity-bearing persistence with spirits. That discussion is fully preserved in the original and in the [source-context record](../../provenance/environment-v0.5.0/SOURCE_CONTEXT_NOTES.md). It is not erased, but is not used to create new reincarnation, soul or consent laws during this environmental repair.
