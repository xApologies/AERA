# Stabilized territory and the Wilds

**Source:** MK147 `KINGDOM_SETTLEMENTS.md`; environmental terminology is inherited, while its civic structures and numerical scales remain optional source examples.

## 1. The Wilds is a risk condition

**The Wilds describes Genesis-risk outside reliable stabilization, not a biome.** It is not simply another word for forest, desert, wilderness or uninhabited land. A map's terrain classification and its Genesis-risk classification answer different questions.

The source describes metropolitan sigil arrays that stabilize the Genesis Field over a region much larger than the urban footprint. In reliably stabilized territory, suppression of Genesis storms/collapse helps support roads, farms, estates, villages and other settlements. Outside that protection, the Wilds classification becomes relevant.

“Suppressed” and “reliable stabilization” are the source's terms. This is not an added promise of absolute safety from every possible environmental, biological or social danger.

## 2. Keep the technologies distinct

Physical `ru’ne` form sigils; coordinated physical sigils form arrays. An Architect-created `velis` is a localized, temporarily persistent Genesis topology rather than a physical inscription. City arrays, agricultural velis and defensive velis can interact in a chosen civilization but are not interchangeable objects.

The existing [Sigils, Architects and velis](../SIGILS_ARCHITECTS_VELIS.md) document remains authoritative for their working definitions. This pass connects that engineering to environmental risk without changing the professions or inventing a new universal barrier mechanism.

## 3. The source's scale example

MK147 gives a large-city array example with four corner sigils and one central sigil acting as a coordinated array, potentially stabilizing on the order of a 200-mile radius. The approximation is preserved as **example scale**, not a mandatory number for all arrays or all cities. It does not follow that every five-sigil array has that coverage.

Likewise, MK147's source hierarchy is Capital City → Large City → Small City → Village Hub, with approximate 10×10-mile large cities, 7×7-mile small cities, a 0.5×0.5-mile initial hub core, and a general 60–80-year maturation plus roughly ten-year redevelopment example. These quantities and institutions belong to that implementation. They are not an AERA settlement law.

## 4. Towns are not Village Hubs

In the source, ordinary towns arise organically from traffic and economic activity; they are not an additional rung of the Crown hierarchy. Dungeon-support settlements similarly serve supply, lodging, staging, logistics and the surrounding delver community. They are distinct from planned Village Hubs.

The source uses “Hollowmere-type” as an example, not a new settlement that must be imported into MK231. Dungeons reaching the higher strata can become major economic anchors without forcing every author to adopt the source's kingdom map.

## 5. Author's implementation freedom

A tribal settlement, independent city, future interplanetary civilization or another social arrangement can use the available environmental mechanisms without inheriting royalty or the source's dimensions. The World Engine belongs to the author. The framework supplies the defined mechanisms and vocabulary, not the completed local society.
