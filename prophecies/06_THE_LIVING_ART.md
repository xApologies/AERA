# VI — The Living Art

*The Sixth Prophecy of Magic*

There shall come a shape that has no maker, and the makers shall gather around it with their instruments and find no place where the first stroke was laid. Its borders will have been crossed before there were borders to cross. Its center will move without departing. What appears finished at dawn shall be unfinished at dusk, though no part of it has been taken away. And those who seek the hand responsible shall discover only the marks left by a thousand hands that never touched it.

The first mistake shall be to call it alive. The second shall be to call it dead. Between these judgments the shape will continue, accepting neither verdict, changing its answer whenever the question is made smaller. A root will divide beneath a stone; a current will divide around an island; a thought will divide within a sleeping mind. None shall borrow the other's name, yet the divisions will resemble one another in ways that trouble the learned. For likeness is not identity, and identity is not the stillness of a thing that refuses to change.

In the days when the heavens are measured by the turning of familiar lights, a garden will be found where no garden was planted. Its branches shall be paths and its paths shall be branches, and the place where they meet will not know which has entered which. What begins as a single line shall return as a boundary; what was a boundary shall open into a chamber; and within that chamber there shall be a smaller wilderness whose edges are farther apart than the edges of the whole. The keepers of maps will draw the chamber again and again. Every drawing will be faithful. No two drawings will agree.

There will be creatures whose bones remember the pressure of ancient seas, though they have never entered water. There will be forests that lean toward a light their leaves cannot see. There will be flowers whose colors answer the stones beneath them and stones that alter beneath the flowers, until neither may be called the beginning of the other. The impatient will name these wonders exceptions. The patient will ask what law has been mistaken for a wall.

And beyond the green country, where no leaf has ever opened, another manner of flourishing shall be permitted. A turning without a wheel. A joining without a seam. A thousand departures from one place, none of them leaving it. The pattern shall grow by returning, and return by growing, and each return shall discover a boundary not present in the last. Those who demand a heart within it shall find none. Those who demand that it be empty shall be unable to explain why its smallest alteration changes the whole.

A name will be offered to the pattern, and the pattern will not become the name. A vessel will be built around it, and the vessel will become part of the thing it was meant to contain. A measure will be placed against its edge, and the edge will pass through the measure. Then the measure will be remade, and the edge will hold still long enough to be counted, though the counting will have changed what is being counted.

There shall be an age when every scholar believes that life is a particular substance, hidden in the blood or sheltered behind the eye. They will sift the substance from the body and seek its smallest portion. They will find smaller portions, each bearing a different arrangement, and beneath each arrangement another relation. At last they will hold a fragment so slight that nothing living can be seen within it. Yet the fragment will still belong to a world of meetings, exchanges, departures, and returns. The answer they sought will not be absent. It will be too widely distributed to rest in the palm.

Nor shall the living be understood by gathering every fragment and laying them in a row. A thousand scattered seeds do not make a forest, and a forest is not diminished to a thousand seeds. Between what is gathered and what grows there shall be an order that cannot be kept in a jar. The order will not be a king issuing commands. It will not be a secret word hidden at the center. It will be the way one thing may alter another without ceasing to be itself, and the way a thing may cease to be itself without bringing the whole to an end.

There shall come a harvest of impossible forms. Some will stand beneath six lights and cast a seventh shadow. Some will drink what others must flee. Some will carry winter beneath their skin and flower only when the air is warm enough to wound them. A creature born under a heavy sky will learn the strength of its ground; another beneath a gentler sky will learn the reach of its branches. The distant worlds shall not agree upon a single body, and the bodies shall not agree upon a single world. Yet no world will have escaped the question of what permits a form to endure.

Do not mistake the six lights for six answers. Their colors shall mingle in the glass of a raindrop and separate in the dark of a buried crystal. The same radiance will nourish one form and unravel another. What is poison in one country shall be bread in the next; what sustains a tree may silence a river. The law of the whole will not be the preference of any part. The art shall not choose a favorite shape.

When the oldest tree falls, a thousand smaller works will begin in its shadow. What has ended as a trunk will continue as soil, shelter, warmth, and hunger. Yet this is not a promise that the tree has survived. The loss will remain a loss. The new forms shall not restore its first branches or return the birds to their former nests. The art is not mercy, though mercy may learn from it; not cruelty, though cruelty may flourish within it. Its abundance shall not absolve its endings.

The question will travel into the places where nothing breathes. A star shall gather itself under burdens that would scatter lesser fires. A storm shall preserve its turning while every drop within it is replaced. A river shall keep a course that has never held the same water twice. A flame shall have a shape only while its substance departs. The watchers will ask which of these is living. The heavens will offer no judgment, and still the forms will move according to their relations.

For there shall be a difference between a thing that changes and a change that keeps a thing. The difference shall be narrow enough to escape a lifetime of searching and broad enough to hold a civilization. A city will remain a city when its walls are rebuilt; a song will remain a song when sung by a thousand voices; a body will remain a body while its hidden materials pass away. But there will come a turning beyond which the name can no longer follow. No bell shall announce the crossing. Those who stand nearest may be the last to recognize it.

And there shall be a more terrible wonder: a form that preserves every mark and cannot grow. No wound shall alter it, no season touch it, no new relation enter its perfect enclosure. It will endure beyond the collapse of kingdoms and the exhaustion of their clocks. Those who fear death will kneel before it. Those who understand gardens will turn away, for an unchanging perfection may be a monument rather than a life.

Another form will admit every change and keep nothing. It will bloom a thousand times in a single instant and leave no trace by which one bloom may be distinguished from the next. Those who worship freedom will praise it. Those who have planted seeds will ask what could ever take root there. Between the unyielding stone and the unremembering flood, a thousand possible arts shall rise, and none shall claim to be the only one.

The smallest chamber shall open upon the largest, though no gate is built between them. A curve shall hold a field, and a field shall hold a curve. The line that encloses will become the line that carries. The point that seems to be an ending will unfold as a place of departure. Those who look for the first direction shall find that the paths have been turning long before their feet arrived. Those who look for the last direction shall find a path bending back toward a question once discarded.

And when the question returns, it will not return unchanged. What was once asked of a leaf shall be asked of a mountain. What was asked of a mountain shall be asked of a thought. What was asked of a thought shall be asked of a world whose inhabitants have never learned the word for thought. Their answer may be sung, carved, measured, or left unspoken. The form of the answer will not decide whether the question has been understood.

There shall be an hour when the makers learn to persuade matter to remember an arrangement it has never held. They will call the work creation. Others will call it imitation. A third will say that the arrangement was waiting to be found. Yet the new thing will have consequences none of its makers foresaw. It will lean toward warmth, cast a shadow, admit a neighbor, and alter the place in which it stands. The art will not be confined to the moment of its making. The world will continue the work.

And a later age shall discover that the hand may be absent altogether. A mark will be set upon a surface, and the surface will answer through its depths. A pattern will be permitted to unfold, not as a command obeyed by every grain, but as a relationship that every grain must encounter. Some grains will join; some will depart; some will become the edge of a thing whose center has not yet appeared. The builders will rejoice at the finished shape, and the shape will already have begun to change the builders.

No form shall be innocent of its surroundings. The smallest living chamber is a meeting of worlds: a boundary that separates, an opening that joins, an interior that cannot remain an interior without an exterior. There shall be exchanges too slight for any eye, and from them forests shall rise. There shall be differences too narrow for any ruler, and from them wings, teeth, roots, and dreams shall take their shape. The great shall depend upon the small without being reducible to it. The small shall belong to the great without being swallowed whole.

Beware the hour when all these differences are mistaken for ranks. The vine does not fail because it cannot walk. The stone does not fail because it cannot remember a face. The creature that sees a hundred colors has not thereby earned a greater right to exist than the creature that knows only warmth and cold. The art is not a ladder with a throne at its summit. Its branches cross, and some descend where others rise. What is called higher from one path shall be called farther from another.

There shall be voices that insist every pattern is a creature and others that insist no pattern is a creature until it speaks. Their quarrel will reach the halls of kings and the mouths of children. Meanwhile the sea will gather a storm, the storm will gather a spiral, and the spiral will endure for a little while before loosening into rain. Neither argument shall delay it. Neither argument shall explain it entirely.

What is it, then, that comes alive when the world begins to shape itself? Not every turning is a birth. Not every stillness is a death. Not every continuity is memory, nor every response an intention. Yet between these denials there remains a wilderness of forms whose boundaries are not yet known. Some will carry awareness. Some will carry only consequence. Some will be encountered once and never recognized again, though they have stood at the threshold of every age.

The garden of the covenant shall touch this wilderness but shall not contain it. One asks what it means for existence to be met; the other shall ask what it means for existence to take form, and to take form again. The questions will share a root without becoming the same branch. The first shall remain unanswered even when every creature is named. The second shall remain unfinished even when every name is forgotten.

And the seventh path shall wait beyond the sixth, though no road shall be laid toward it. The forms will cast maps upon one another, and the maps will acquire edges their makers did not intend. A boundary will be crossed by something that never traveled. A distant chamber will become near without the intervening places being destroyed. The art will be mistaken for a key, a door, a vessel, a language. It may wear each likeness for an hour and surrender each before the hour is spent.

At the last turning there shall be no finished portrait, no perfect garden, no creature standing as the final proof. There shall be a space left open within the pattern, not because the pattern has failed, but because no final shape has been commanded of it. Across that opening the scattered lines shall approach, part, and approach again. What has no voice shall nevertheless be heard in its effects. What has no eyes shall nevertheless alter what can be seen. What has no life shall not thereby be without form, and what has life shall not thereby be understood.

Then the art shall remain where it has always been: neither in the hand alone nor in the substance alone, neither in the first boundary nor the last, but in the unfinished relation by which a world may become other than it was and still be a world. And where the final line should close, it shall turn—not outward, not inward, but toward a place that has not yet been given a direction.
