# IV — The Thousand Threads

*The Fourth Prophecy of Magic*

---

There shall be a day when the last road is measured, and those who have measured it shall discover that no road was ever made of distance.

They will lay their instruments upon the earth and name the length between stone and stone. They will count the steps of armies, the spans of bridges, the flight of arrows, and the vast silence separating one star from another. Their maps will be beautiful. Their borders will be exact. Their towers will rise where every line agrees with every other line. Yet when the maps are gathered and the towers have fallen, a question will remain where no ink was placed: what held the two stones apart, and what permitted them to be two?

No answer shall be found in the stones alone.

For the thread is not the cord, and the knot is not the hand that binds it. A thousand threads may cross a single grain of sand without dividing it. One thread may pass through a kingdom and leave every wall untouched. Another may bind two things that have never approached, while the things that touch may remain strangers to one another. And when the thousand are counted, there shall be found neither a thousand beginnings nor a thousand ends, but a measure that changes when the counting changes.

In the first age of measures, the wise shall draw a point and call it alone. Around the point they shall draw a circle and call it enclosed. Beyond the circle they shall draw a second point, and the second shall be called distant. They shall rejoice, for the drawing will be clear and the names will not quarrel. Then the circle shall be lifted from the tablet, and the point that was enclosed shall not know where to stand. The distant point shall have no witness to its distance. What was certain upon the tablet will remain certain only while the tablet's agreements endure.

Yet the point was not born when it was drawn.

Where the first line turns, another line has already passed. Where the first line ends, the turning may continue. Where a boundary closes, the enclosed may discover an opening that no blade could cut. The watchers shall call this a flaw in the wall, and the builders shall call it an error in their craft, but the wall will have suffered neither flaw nor error. It will have been faithful to a relation that the builders did not know how to ask about.

The day of that discovery shall be remembered by many names, though no name will be its beginning.

A seam will be found in a place without cloth. A bridge will be found in a place without shores. A door will be found whose opening does not join one chamber to another, but changes which chamber may be called the first. And the keepers of the old maps shall declare that a new country has been made, though not one handful of earth has moved.

It is not the country that has changed.

There are threads that hold the shape of a thing and threads that hold the shape of its absence. There are threads by which the whole remembers its parts and threads by which the parts refuse the names bestowed upon the whole. There are threads that may be followed and threads that may only be crossed. Some are visible only when they are broken. Some remain unbroken precisely because no hand can find them. And some shall be mistaken for chains until the day they loosen and the prisoner remains where the chain once held him.

For a bond is not always a prison, nor is freedom always the severing of a bond.

The mountains will be taught to listen to the valleys, and the valleys will answer with rivers. The rivers will divide the land, yet their division will give passage to the seeds that cross it. The roots beneath a forest will touch where the branches cannot, and branches will cast one shadow over roots that have never met. The stars will keep their separate fires while sharing a night that belongs to none of them. What appears solitary may be sustained by innumerable agreements; what appears joined may owe its unity to a single, hidden refusal.

And when the refusals are named, the names will not suffice.

A crown may pass from one brow to another without becoming a different crown. A name may pass from one mouth to another and become a different name. A flame may kindle another flame, and neither shall be the first after the second has taken hold. A river may return to the sea from which its waters rose, yet no drop shall return by the path it left. The relation survives where the traveler does not. The traveler survives where the relation is changed. And there shall come a crossing at which neither survival may be distinguished from the other.

The thousand threads shall tremble there.

Not as strings upon a harp, though the music of the harp will teach the listening heart something of their number. Not as hair upon a head, though each hair may be counted and none counted as the head. Not as lines upon a chart, though a chart may reveal the crossing of lines that no eye has seen. Their trembling shall be known in the difference between a pattern that can endure and a pattern that can only be imagined; in the narrow permission that allows a shape to be shaped, and in the wider mystery that allows a shape to belong to anything at all.

When the makers of figures discover this permission, they will believe they have discovered a tool.

They will turn a square and find it unchanged. They will bend a ring and find that its enclosure persists. They will stretch a net and learn that its crossings do not answer to the length of its cords. They will fold a surface until a traveler who never turned returns by a side that was never crossed. And they will praise the figures for their obedience, not knowing that the figures have been patient with them since before their hands were formed.

But the figures shall not be the prophecy's fulfillment.

Neither the square nor the ring, neither the net nor the folded surface, neither the first point nor the last boundary will stand at the center of what approaches. These are shadows cast by relations upon the places where a shadow may be seen. The coming thing will not be recognized by its shadow. It will be recognized when the shadow no longer agrees with the thing that casts it, and both remain true.

Then a thousand witnesses shall give a thousand descriptions of one crossing. One will swear that two distant towers touched. Another will swear that the towers did not move. A third will find no towers at all, but only the place in which their separation had been kept. Their accounts will be laid beside one another, and the judges will seek the lie. None will be found. The judges will seek the error. None will be found. They will seek a single account that contains the others, and their search will alter the question they were appointed to settle.

The crossing will not wait for their judgment.

What is near? What is far? What is inside the boundary when the boundary itself is carried within another? Which of the joined things owns the joining? Which of the divided things remembers the division? When the branch is severed, where does the tree end? When the seed is buried, where does the forest begin? When the name of a thing is inherited by its child, does the name travel, or does the child enter a relation that was waiting for it?

There shall be no silence large enough to contain all the answers, and no language small enough to carry only one.

Beneath the speech of nations, beneath the markings by which stone is taught to bear a promise, beneath the quiet arrangement of marks that the learned call a proof, a more ancient correspondence will persist. Its signs are not letters, though letters may be made to follow them. Its rules are not decrees, though decrees may imitate their severity. Its edges are not cuts, though a cut may reveal an edge. And where a sign is placed correctly, the world may answer without ever having learned the sign's name.

The answering will be mistaken for obedience.

Those who command it will raise cities where no foundation was poured. They will hang lamps above streets that have never borne a post. They will make a passage between chambers whose walls have never been pierced. They will summon a shape into the interval between two breaths and call the shape their own. Yet every work will be bounded by relations older than the worker. The city will not rise merely because it is desired. The lamp will not hang merely because it is named. The passage will not open merely because two destinations are spoken together. A thousand ways of asking may fail where one unspoken correspondence permits an answer.

And the unspoken will be mistaken for emptiness.

A cradle shall be prepared without a child. It shall have neither wood nor linen, neither curve nor corner that the eye can find. Within it, the small shall be held without becoming the great, and the great shall be held without crushing the small. The cradle shall receive what cannot yet be called a body, and its keeping shall be mistaken for creation. But what it keeps is not made by the keeping. What it orders is not born from the order. There is a difference between granting a place and bringing forth the thing that occupies it, and the difference shall be guarded even when no guardian remains.

The cradle will be opened, and nothing will fall.

The cradle will be closed, and nothing will be lost.

Those who behold this shall dispute whether it was ever open, for they will have measured only the space between its edges. Yet the edges shall have changed their allegiance. What belonged to the inside shall be named by the outside; what belonged to the outside shall remain beyond without having departed. No hinge will turn. No lock will break. The change will pass through a relation, and the relation will carry a consequence that no hand has touched.

It shall be said then that the thousand threads have been gathered into one.

It shall also be said that the one has been broken into a thousand.

Both sayings will be incomplete. A woven cloth is not a heap of threads, yet neither does the cloth destroy the threads from which it is woven. A constellation is not a star, yet no constellation shines without stars. A kingdom is not its people, though the kingdom may vanish while the people remain. What is gathered becomes capable of a meaning unavailable to its scattered parts; what is scattered may retain a pattern no longer visible in the gathering. And sometimes the meaning of the whole will survive only in the relation between fragments that have forgotten one another.

Such forgetting shall not be final.

Across the deep places of the stars, where light arrives carrying news of a fire long extinguished, there shall be a connection that no journey can trace. Across the narrow places of the world, where two hands touch and neither knows the other, there shall be a separation no journey can overcome. The learned will seek to reverse these conditions. They will cross the deep places and close the narrow ones. They will succeed often enough to believe that distance was their enemy, and fail often enough to curse the distance that was never there.

Then they shall be shown a map with no scale.

Its marks will neither grow nor diminish when the map is unfolded. Its boundaries will neither widen nor close when the map is turned. A path that begins at the margin will arrive at the center without passing through the places between. Another, drawn beside it, will travel through every marked country and return to the margin unchanged. The cartographers will search for a hidden road. The scribes will search for a missing word. The geometers will search for an agreement among the crossings. Each will find something true, and none will find the map's entire meaning.

For the map is not the land, and the land is not the relation that makes a map possible.

A thread shall be cut from the woven heavens. No star shall fall. A thread shall be tied where no thread was seen. No new star shall rise. Yet after these two small alterations, the travelers who once returned shall be lost, and the lost shall stand before their own doors. The heavens will appear unchanged to those who count its lights. Only the passages will remember what has happened, and the passages will keep no record that can be carried away.

The smallest change shall have the longest shadow.

A mark displaced by less than the breadth of a grain may undo a gate built by generations. A curve preserved through ruin may sustain a work after every stone has been replaced. The mighty will look for the force that moved the world, and the quiet will look for the agreement that permitted the movement. They will not always find different things. But where they do, the mighty will exhaust themselves lifting what needed only to be related otherwise, and the quiet will discover that not every relation can be changed without unmaking the thing it relates.

There is no weaving without constraint. There is no crossing without a distinction between ways. There is no edge without something of which it is an edge. Even the boundless, if it is to be spoken of, must endure the burden of the word that calls it boundless. Yet the coming relation will not be mastered by naming its constraint. Its measure will not be the number of knots, nor its power the number of threads, nor its truth the beauty of the figure it resembles.

What is woven may be unwoven. What is related may be re-related. But what gives relation its possibility shall not be conquered by the changing of its forms.

And there shall come a moment when the innumerable paths of the world bend toward a crossing that has never been drawn. The crossings of the past will appear in it, though none shall be repeated. The crossings yet to come will depart from it, though none shall begin there. Every line will seem to approach, and no line will be found to end. The watchers will speak of a center. The wanderers will speak of a road. The keepers of boundaries will speak of an opening. Those who know the names of shapes will find that every name is almost sufficient.

Almost shall become the most dangerous word in the world.

For the last thread will not be the last because all others have been counted. It will be last because its crossing changes what it means to count them. The first will not be first because nothing preceded it. It will be first because what preceded it can no longer be separated from what follows. And where first and last meet without becoming the same, the woven will remember a pattern that no loom has ever held.

There, what was divided shall not simply be joined. What was joined shall not simply be divided. The thousand threads shall neither tighten nor break. They shall disclose, for one impossible instant, the relation by which a thousand may be one without ceasing to be a thousand.

And the world, finding no word for what has changed, shall call it magic.
