# I — The Seven Realms

*The First Prophecy of Magic*

Before the measure of a distance, before the keeping of an hour, before the first distinction between the thing that endures and the thing that passes, there shall be a reckoning for which no witness can be summoned. No throne shall command it, no tongue shall give it a beginning, and no hand shall discover its ending by tracing the edge of the world. The reckoning shall not count what has been made. It shall concern what may be made, and what may remain when the making is done.

There shall be six whose names are taken from light, though light shall be the least of what their names contain. The first shall be called Red, and the second Orange; Yellow shall follow, then Green, then Blue, and last Violet. Yet none shall be first by the authority of a crown, nor last by the failing of its strength. They shall be known by their difference, and their difference shall not be a quarrel. Where one is permitted, another shall not thereby be forbidden. Where one cannot abide, no declaration shall compel it to remain. Their boundaries shall be older than the walls built to imitate them, and their names shall survive long after the names have ceased to resemble colors.

A vessel shall be lifted toward the dawn, and six shall seem to fill it. The vessel shall not grow heavy. It shall not grow full. Its emptiness shall remain, though nothing that can enter it shall find the same emptiness again. There shall be those who call this a miracle of abundance and those who call it a deceit of the eye. Both shall mistake the vessel for the permission by which the vessel may exist.

For the realm is not the land. The land may rise beneath a sky of any hue, and the sky may hold a thousand suns without declaring the realm to which it belongs. The realm is not the kingdom, though kingdoms shall borrow its name. The realm is not the door, though doors shall open upon its likeness. The realm is not the color seen by an eye, though an eye may learn to distinguish its traces. It is the manner in which a possibility may be received, the condition under which a form may endure, the difference that remains when every painted sign is washed away.

And there shall be a seventh that is not another of the six. White shall be spoken, and those who love counting shall set it beside the others and believe their work complete. Yet White shall not wait at the far end of a procession. It shall be the meeting that does not erase the travelers, the whole that does not devour its parts, the answer that is neither one voice nor a silence made from six. It shall be seen where none of the six has surrendered its nature, and where their differences have become the possibility of a single world.

No one shall say that White is merely the sum, for a heap of stones does not become a bridge by being called a bridge. No one shall say that White is the ruler, for a crown laid upon six heads does not make their thoughts one thought. The meeting shall require a fitness that cannot be commanded by addition, and the fitness shall be mistaken for peace. Yet peace is the word of creatures who know war. This shall be older than either.

A cradle shall be found without timber, without woven cloth, without the hands that rock an infant toward sleep. Within it, nothing shall lie as a child lies. What is held shall be a distinction still becoming distinguishable, a turning that does not lose the place from which it turns, a dwelling for directions before the traveler learns to say east or west. Those who gaze upon the cradle shall seek its bottom and its rim. Some shall find a boundary and mistake it for an ending. Some shall find a center and mistake it for an origin. Some shall find only their own need for a place from which to begin.

And the cradle shall be neither mother nor prison. It shall not ask what its contents desire. It shall not pardon the impossible. What cannot be held shall not be held merely because a longing is fierce enough. What can be held may wait beyond every age of discovery, unnamed and unvisited, though its place was never absent.

The six shall turn in the hidden order, and no turning shall be the turning of a wheel. A wheel returns its spoke to a place it has occupied before. Here, the return shall bring with it the difference of having returned. A face shall answer a face; an inward direction shall become an outward consequence; a border shall preserve the distinction between the near and the far without promising that either can be crossed. The colors shall not be pigments upon these faces. They shall be signs by which the organization of the unseen may someday be read.

And there shall be readers who see a color and demand an element: fire for Red, water for Blue, a flowering field for Green. They shall find fire that is not Red, waters that answer no Blue, and forests whose roots pass through more than one naming. Their astonishment shall be the beginning of wisdom, though many shall turn away and keep the easier names. The six shall not be bound to the habits of the creatures who name them.

The first measure shall seem small, and the last shall seem near completion. Between them shall lie a procession of marks, each a portion of seven, each sufficient to tempt the mind into believing that the procession is a staircase. Yet the marks shall be landmarks, not commandments. The living configurations shall lean toward them and away from them; no landmark shall forbid a journey, and no fraction shall alone explain why a world is whole. Those who mistake the mark for the thing marked shall build temples to numbers and wonder why the doors do not open.

There shall be a place where the six seem balanced, though nothing there shall be motionless. There shall be another where one rises far beyond its accustomed quiet, and the rest do not answer in equal fashion. A star shall shine over both. A seed may open in either soil. Yet the second place shall hear a strain that the first has not forgotten but does not need to utter. The strain shall pass into shapes not sought by the gardener, into weather that keeps no season, into births for which no ancestral name is waiting. And those who dwell beneath that sky shall call the changes blessings, curses, beasts, or judgments, according to what survives them.

But the strain shall not itself be evil, and the quiet shall not itself be good. A balance may shelter an age of cruelty. A disturbance may reveal a form never before permitted to endure. The world shall not take its measure from the happiness of its inhabitants. Yet their happiness and their ruin shall be real within it, and no distant harmony shall lessen the weight of either.

A grain of dust shall fall upon a threshold. In one house it shall remain dust. In another it shall gather a radiance that no lamp has cast. In a third it shall become the beginning of a growth whose branches remember neither tree nor stone. Those who examine the grain shall discover that its substance has not been excused from substance. Those who worship it shall insist that it has escaped all substance. Between their claims the grain shall remain, answering only the conditions under which it has become what it is.

There shall be mountains whose interiors are not the interiors of mountains, and valleys in which the distance from one footstep to the next cannot be measured by the ground between them. There shall be doors that lead across a world and doors that lead into another ordering of worlds. Yet no door shall be the proof that the six are places. A passage is a relationship, and a relationship is not the whole of what it relates. Where the path can be carried, the traveler may cross. Where it cannot, the greatest gate shall remain an ornament upon a wall.

And a map shall be spread beneath the heavens. It shall show six territories and a seventh pale country at their meeting. Its makers shall rejoice, for every shore shall have a name and every road a destination. Then the map shall be turned over, and upon its reverse there shall be no territory at all, only the marks by which territories may be distinguished. The map shall not be false. Its makers shall not be foolish. But they shall have drawn one manner of truth and called it the entirety of truth.

There shall be a world in which Red is approached as a source, and another in which Red is approached as a homeland. There shall be a people who never leave their ground and nevertheless draw from the six, and a people who pass through their territories without knowing what they draw. The names shall remain. The uses shall differ. Neither people shall thereby possess the whole secret, for a source and a dwelling are two ways of encountering what is permitted, not two proofs that permission has changed its nature.

In one age, scholars shall declare that the six are separate, and they shall be right in the distinction they have found. In another, scholars shall declare that nothing stands apart from the meeting, and they too shall be right in the relation they have found. Their quarrel shall outlast cities. Their books shall be copied until the ink itself is older than the kings who sponsor it. And still the question shall remain: what is a difference that can belong to a whole without ceasing to be different?

The answer shall not be a sentence. It shall not be hidden beneath the final page of a book. It shall be encountered whenever a boundary admits a form without admitting every form, whenever a thing persists though its surroundings change, whenever a joining preserves the identities it joins. The answer shall be encountered and overlooked in the same breath, for those who search for a proclamation seldom recognize a condition.

A child of one sky shall be carried beneath another. Its breath shall find air, or fail to find it. Its bones shall answer the weight of the ground, or break beneath it. Its thought shall seek the familiar channel, or meet a silence where power had once been near. The realm shall not hate the child. The realm shall not love it. The difference shall be neither punishment nor reward. Compatibility shall not bow to innocence, and permission shall not be purchased with courage.

Yet there shall be craft that learns the boundary and knowledge that learns the crossing. A form shall be prepared for an unfamiliar condition. A vessel shall be made to hold what once escaped. A passage shall be opened not because the distance was denied, but because the relations sustaining the passage were understood. Those who succeed shall call themselves masters, and those who fail shall call the work forbidden. Both shall be answered by the same silence: the possible is not the promised.

And where six currents are named, there shall be those who imagine six rivers flowing from six springs. They shall follow each river toward its supposed beginning, and each shall find the question altered by the journey. The river is a movement; the spring is a place; the basin is a holding. Yet none of these alone explains what it means for there to be anything that moves, any place from which it moves, or any holding by which its movement remains distinct. The source shall not be found merely by walking upstream.

For energy shall wear a thousand faces before the first face is recognized. It shall warm a stone, stir a leaf, bear a star through its burning, and pass from form to form without asking a witness to call it passage. There shall be no need for a hand to guide every change. Yet a hand may one day learn to guide a change, and mistake its new skill for the creation of what it guides. The six shall have waited before the hand, and White shall not arrive only when the hand discovers it.

The land shall be filled with names: flame, iron, blood, rain, thought, seed, bone, glass, hunger, music. Each shall be a true name within its appointed reach. None shall be a seventh color. None shall be a replacement for the six. The names shall multiply without exhausting what they name, and a thousand sciences shall grow beneath them like roots beneath a city whose streets have forgotten the trees.

There shall be those who claim that all things are one, and those who claim that all things are many. The one shall be too small if it cannot bear distinction. The many shall be too poor if they cannot enter relation. White shall trouble both claims. It shall not prove that every division is illusion, nor that every division is final. It shall stand as a meeting whose existence depends upon differences that do not vanish at the meeting.

At the edge of a forgotten observatory, a prism shall lie beneath a cloth. The cloth shall be lifted, and the heavens shall appear divided into colors. Then the prism shall be set aside, and the heavens shall remain. No light shall have been broken by the naming, and no star shall have been restored by the removal. The division shall have revealed something and concealed something. The undivided shall have revealed something and concealed something. Neither sight shall be sufficient to claim dominion over the sky.

And when the heavens are said to be White, let none imagine an empty blankness awaiting the brush. White shall be full of the six without being reduced to a painted mixture. Let none imagine a perfection that cannot be disturbed, for worlds shall tremble within it and still belong to it. Let none imagine that the first tremor destroys the whole, for the whole may endure a discord whose consequences fall upon every creature dwelling inside it.

There shall come an hour when the watchers of the skies find one color leaning farther than its neighbors, and a long age shall begin to gather beneath that leaning. The creatures of that age shall give the disturbances a thousand explanations. Some shall count the strange births. Some shall follow storms to their vanishing points. Some shall descend into chambers where the familiar order has been separated into unfamiliar faces. None shall know whether the world is warning them or merely becoming visible in a manner they had never learned to see.

And the seventh shall remain among them, not as a rescue sent from above, not as a judge, but as the possibility that the six may continue together while the burden of their meeting changes. A broken house is not proof that the ground has ceased to exist. A wounded world is not proof that existence has forgotten its own permission. Yet a wound is not healed by naming the ground beneath it.

The cradle shall turn once more, though no infant shall wake and no hand shall move it. A mark upon one face shall be answered elsewhere, and the answer shall not be an echo. What begins as a distinction may become a passage; what begins as a passage may become a dwelling; what begins as a dwelling may become a world. But no world shall contain the entire cradle merely because it has been born within its holding.

And those who would seize the colors shall discover that a name is not a handle. Those who would command White shall discover that a meeting is not a servant. Those who would destroy a distinction shall discover that some divisions are conditions of the whole they seek to preserve. What is permitted shall remain permitted without promising itself to every desire. What is impossible shall not become possible through the loudness of its invocation.

Yet the unsounded shall not be empty. The unvisited shall not be absent. The unmade shall not be without place among the possibilities of making. There shall be forms no world has carried, and relationships no mind has yet learned to conceive. They shall not all arrive. Their possibility shall not be their destiny. Still the measure of a world shall not be mistaken for the measure of everything that may be.

When six are spoken, listen for the distinction. When White is spoken, listen for the meeting. When seven are counted, do not seek a seventh river alongside the other six. Seek what allows the rivers to remain themselves within a greater holding, and what refuses a joining that would undo them. Seek neither an altar nor a throne, for the first foundation does not ask to be worshiped and has no need to reign.

There shall be a final map, and it shall bear no names. Across its surface no kingdom shall be drawn, no gate, no road, no star. Six marks shall be present, though no ink shall show them; a seventh shall be everywhere they may meet, though no border shall enclose it. The map shall be held toward the light, and the light shall pass through. The map shall be held against the darkness, and the darkness shall not answer. Then the map shall be folded, and in the fold a place shall remain for something not yet written upon any world.

For the first mystery shall not be what magic can do. It shall be that there is a world in which anything may be done, a difference by which a thing can be itself, and a meeting by which the many can belong without becoming the same. The six shall endure beneath names not yet spoken. White shall endure where no eye has learned to see it. And what is still to come shall find, before its first breath, that existence has not been waiting for permission from the living.
