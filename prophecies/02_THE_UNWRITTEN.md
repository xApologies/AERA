# II — THE UNWRITTEN

*The Second Prophecy of Magic*

Before the first name, there shall be no silence, for silence itself shall not yet have been divided from sound. Before the first mark, the stone shall carry a thousand possible inscriptions, and none shall belong to the hand that will discover them. The first word shall be spoken, and the world shall not answer. The second shall be spoken, and the world shall appear to answer. Upon this difference shall rise the towers of an age, and beneath those towers shall sleep the question that no tower can contain.

The measure shall precede the measuring. No circle shall have been drawn, yet its turning shall endure. No distance shall have been counted, yet the interval shall wait. No vessel shall have received the waters, yet the waters shall know the curve of their vessel. What is named shall not begin when it is named. What is forgotten shall not necessarily perish when its name is lost. And the nameless shall remain where the last inscription has weathered into dust.

Then shall the sand fall upon the tablet.

It shall fall without instruction. A thousand grains shall find the edges of a mark no eye can see. Another thousand shall rest upon the smoothness beside it. The tablet shall be called empty before the falling and full afterward; yet the empty tablet shall possess the divisions, and the full tablet shall conceal them. Those who seek the answer in the sand shall gather the grains into heaps. Those who seek it in the stone shall sweep the grains away. Neither shall notice that the question has changed while the tablet has remained still.

The mark shall not be the meaning. The meaning shall not be the mark. Yet they shall meet, and at their meeting a thing not visible in either alone shall become possible. A line shall be cut in iron and another in glass; their shapes shall appear the same, and their answers shall differ. A figure shall be broken and still endure in its consequence. A flawless figure shall remain mute. The makers shall accuse the materials, the materials shall remain innocent, and the truth shall not be found in the accusation.

For no hand invents the permission of a boundary. The hand may discover it, misread it, give it a shape, or fail to hold it. The figure that cannot bear its own consequence shall not be saved by the beauty of its inscription. The crooked mark may preserve a relation that the perfect copy has lost. Neither straightness nor ornament shall be crowned as law. The admissible shall not be determined by applause, nor the impossible made possible by the fervor of a multitude.

There shall be an age that worships its alphabets. It shall bind its knowledge in books and name each book complete. There shall be an age that despises books and seeks the truth in the living voice alone. A third shall carve its answers into mountains, trusting stone to outlast the frailty of tongues. All three shall inherit something. All three shall mistake inheritance for possession. Their words shall be carried beyond the borders of their kingdoms, altered by mouths that never knew their authors, until solemn utterances become ordinary laughter, anger, blessing, and contempt. The old words shall endure without the old understanding. Yet the relation once named shall neither grow old nor be made young by their forgetfulness.

A language beneath languages shall be sought. It shall not be a hidden alphabet that translates every tongue. It shall not be a single word whose speaking opens every gate. Its utterances may be distances, its consonants boundaries, its silences relations that no throat can shape. Those who dwell among stars shall call it a path. Those who dwell beneath stone shall call it an edge. Those who have never counted shall know its turning without giving it a number. They shall disagree, and their disagreement shall not prove that all have found different things.

What is one when no second has been conceived? What is nothing when no boundary has been drawn around absence? The smallest interval shall contain distinctions beyond the patience of any counter. The boundless shall be approached through a boundary, and the whole may be represented within a part without becoming that part. Those who mistake a representation for the thing represented shall build their wonders upon a fragile foundation. Those who reject every representation shall remain unable to tell another what they have discovered. Between the two shall lie an inheritance neither can own.

The circle shall return where no circle was expected. It shall be found in the turning of a star, in the curve of a cup, in the enclosing of a thought, in the refusal of a path to possess an end. Yet these shall not be the same circle, and no likeness shall abolish their differences. The world shall be full of resemblances that do not grant permission. A relation may be true within one boundary and incoherent within another. A name may survive while the thing it names is transformed. The ancient shall not become universal merely because it is ancient.

And there shall be those who remember fire only by its color. They shall imagine its brightness and ask that brightness to burn. Others shall know the hunger of flame, the passage of heat, the exchange of matter, the breath by which the burning is sustained. Their understanding shall carry them farther, but neither shall own the flame. For a likeness is not a transformation, and a transformation is not a likeness; the one may be seen without the other, and the other may be guided without ever being fully seen.

The first intention shall not vanish when the first form is made. It shall travel with the forming, remain within the holding, and return when what was held is asked to become. Thus the beginning shall wait at the ending, not as a memory of the beginning but as a demand the ending must answer. Six motions shall be named where a single wonder is witnessed. Their names shall be remembered by those who cannot perform them and forgotten by those whose hands know their order. The motions shall not become six sovereign powers. They shall remain one passage, and the passage shall be greater than its recitation.

Yet the world shall transform without intention. Heat shall pass into motion, matter into new arrangements, and the stars shall neither pause for an observer nor wait to be told their names. The marvel shall not be that change exists. The marvel shall be that intention may enter change and guide a possibility toward a consequence. Even then, the guided shall not become the property of the guide, nor the possible cease to have boundaries merely because a will has touched it.

And what of the form held behind the eyes, before stone receives it? Shall it be less because it is unseen? Shall it be greater because no substance has yet refused it? The uninscribed shall trouble both judgments. Some imaginings shall never bear their own consequences. Some shall find expression through a structure discovered long before their dreamer was born. Some shall pass from mind to mind without ever taking the shape their first conceiver expected. And some shall be lost, though the world will continue to permit them.

The scholars shall seek the grammar and find fragments of it. The artisans shall seek the useful and discover truths their scholars cannot yet name. The untaught shall feel currents that learned hands cannot touch. The learned shall perceive boundaries that feeling alone cannot sustain. No crown shall be given to ignorance; no crown shall be given to knowledge. Their meeting shall reveal a third uncertainty, and the third shall not be settled by calling it mastery.

Where a boundary is moved, the things on either side may remain unmoved, yet the meaning of within and without shall change. Where a name is altered, the named may endure, yet the obligations of those who speak the name shall not remain the same. Where a relation is discovered, what seemed distant may be near without having crossed any distance. Thus the winding path shall return to its first crossing, and those who follow it shall find that they have come back to a place they have never occupied before.

The sand shall lie upon the tablet again. This time no figure shall be sought. The grains shall gather in an unbroken groove, and the groove shall point toward a place where the stone bears no incision. There the observers shall expect another mark, and there they shall find none. The absence shall be mistaken for a failure. Later it shall be called a command. Later still it shall be understood that neither failure nor command had been present. The space was necessary, though no hand had cut it.

There shall come a time when every name is remembered and the meaning of naming is forgotten. The great inscriptions shall be read backward; the old geometries shall be turned upon their sides; the final tablets shall be broken and their fragments mistaken for beginnings. Each reading shall uncover a relation, and each shall conceal another. The interpreters shall declare the revelation complete, and completion shall be the one word their revelation cannot bear.

For the unwritten shall not be a missing book, nor a forbidden syllable, nor a secret kept in the mouths of the dead. It shall be the possibility that a truth may be expressed without being exhausted; that a structure may be discovered without being possessed; that meaning may survive its language and a language may survive its meaning. It shall be the grammar of what has not yet found a voice, and of what shall remain when every voice has gone.

When the final grain has settled, the tablet shall be lifted. Beneath it shall be no second tablet, no hidden scripture, no answer waiting in the earth. There shall be only the impression left where the stone had rested, exact in its boundary and empty of its weight. And in that emptiness the countless marks that might have been written shall remain possible.

Then shall the world learn to read what was never written.
