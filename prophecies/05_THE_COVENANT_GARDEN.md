# V — The Covenant Garden

*The Fifth Prophecy of Magic*

Before the garden was called a garden, there was no gate by which to enter it, no wall by which to know its bounds, and no hand to say that anything within it had begun. The dark was neither empty nor full. The stars kept their distances without counting them. The waters descended without knowing what it meant to fall. Beneath the unmoving mountains, a thousand changes passed unremembered, and not one was less complete for having no witness.

Yet there shall come a season in which the question is planted where no seed has been laid: if no eye receives the dawn, upon what does morning break? If no ear gathers the thunder, where does its ending go? If the last living breath is surrendered and no other breath follows, does the world become silent, or does silence lose the one who might have named it?

No answer shall be carved upon the first stone. No answer shall be carried by the last. Between them the garden shall open, though nothing shall have opened it.

There shall be roots beneath roots, and under those roots neither soil nor darkness, but a place where holding and being held cannot be distinguished. A leaf shall drink from what has never seen a leaf. A river shall carry the mountain without diminishing its height. The mountain shall give its shadow to the river and never ask for its return. Those who arrive with ledgers shall call this exchange; those who arrive with crowns shall call it tribute; those who arrive with knives shall call it conquest. The garden shall answer none of them, and still every root shall grow according to what reaches it.

For the covenant is older than the promise and stranger than obedience. It has no signature, and no witness stands outside it. It is not made when two voices agree. It is not broken when one voice falls silent. It is found in the opening that permits another thing to enter, and in the boundary that keeps that opening from becoming an ending.

A door may be closed and yet admit the winter. A shell may be sealed and yet bear the sea within it. A seed may remain buried for an age, neither dead nor awake in any language spoken above it, until a warmth without a name passes through its hidden chambers. Then what was one becomes a multitude, and the multitude does not know whether it has divided or remembered.

There shall be those who say that life is the flame and magic the oil, and others who swear that magic is the flame and life the vessel. Their arguments shall outlast cities. The flame shall not wait for either account. In the ash a green point shall appear, and those who believed they had witnessed an ending shall discover that the word *ending* was too small for what had occurred.

But no leaf shall be commanded to signify hope. No flower shall be required to announce rebirth. The garden does not bend toward the meanings given to it. It grows across them. It enters the cracks between their stones. Its smallest inhabitants shall alter kingdoms without knowing that kingdoms exist, and the greatest among its trees shall perish for want of something that a child could hold between two fingers.

Where the first boundary is drawn, the question shall return in another guise. What separates the living from the place in which it lives? The skin from the wind? The blood from the water that preceded it? The thought from the silence before thought? Every answer shall draw a line, and every line shall touch what it excludes. No boundary shall be free of relation. No relation shall be free of the possibility of change.

And there shall be a day when the makers of measures discover that the smallest garden has no single edge. They shall follow a vein into a leaf, the leaf into a branch, the branch into a root, the root into a stone, the stone into the rain, and the rain into a cloud beyond the mountain. At each crossing they shall declare that the garden has ended. At each crossing the garden shall continue. When they return to their first mark, they shall find it covered with moss, and the moss shall not know that it has defeated a definition.

Some shall call the world a vessel in which life is carried. Some shall call life a vessel in which the world is known. The vessel shall be turned, and neither its inside nor its outside shall remain where the turning began. What had been held shall become the holder; what had been sheltered shall become the shelter. No voice shall announce the turning, though the turning shall be heard in every place where a living thing reaches toward another.

The garden shall not be innocent. It shall bloom upon ruin. It shall feed upon what once had a name. The hunter shall enter the body of the hunted, and the hunted shall enter the soil, and the soil shall rise again through the hunger of the hunter. Those who demand purity shall find no unbroken circle. Those who demand justice from every root shall find that the roots have no court. Yet there shall be obligations written in neither law nor mercy: what is taken changes the taker; what is given changes the giver; what survives carries the shapes of what did not.

Beware the promise that nothing may be lost. A garden in which nothing falls cannot become a garden. Its flowers shall remain unopened, its waters unspent, its paths untouched by feet, until its perfect preservation is indistinguishable from stone. Beware also the promise that everything must be surrendered. Where no form endures, no meeting can occur. Between the two promises lies a narrow place, and no ruler shall keep it narrow by decree.

It shall be said that a thing lives because it moves. Then the mountain shall move too slowly to be accused. It shall be said that a thing lives because it grows. Then the crystal shall raise its patient walls. It shall be said that a thing lives because it remembers. Then the river shall return each spring by a course no hand has taught it. It shall be said that a thing lives because it knows itself. Then a sleeping infant shall trouble the scholars, and the forest shall remain unasked.

There shall be no final word upon the threshold. The threshold shall remain, and things shall cross it.

Not every crossing shall be seen. Far beneath the garden, where no root has reached and no breath can descend, the unseen shall change. The change shall touch the stone; the stone shall touch the water; the water shall touch the living; the living shall touch the question. And the question shall travel farther than any traveler, though it shall have neither feet nor destination.

When the distant lights are counted, some shall ask whether the garden ends at the last inhabited world. Others shall ask whether every world is a garden whose season has not yet come. Neither shall hear the answer in the counting. The lights will burn before the first arrival and after the final departure. But somewhere among them, a thing shall open toward another thing, and a difference will arise between a universe that contains a flame and a universe in which a flame is known.

That difference shall be mistaken for the whole of magic. It is not the whole. It shall be mistaken for nothing at all. It is not nothing. It is the interval between an occurrence and its arrival in a living world; between a change and the one changed by it; between the unasked question and the mouth that cannot keep from asking.

There shall be creatures who drink the light without seeing it, and creatures who see it without drinking it. There shall be minds that shape what their hands cannot touch, and bodies that alter their surroundings without a thought resembling a word. Some shall reach into the hidden current deliberately. Others shall be carried by it from birth to dissolution. The current shall not favor the clever, nor shall it deny the unknowing. Its passage shall be different in each, and the difference shall be neither rank nor verdict.

The gardener who believes every living thing must possess a garden shall build walls until no wind enters. The gardener who believes every living thing must be free of all walls shall scatter the seeds upon bare rock. Both shall wonder why the green does not obey. Yet in a broken vessel forgotten beside the road, rain shall gather. A single root shall find the crack. Nothing shall have consented in words, and still a relation shall have become possible.

The covenant shall not be reduced to permission. Permission may be given and withdrawn. The covenant spoken here is the mystery of participation itself: how one thing enters the continuance of another without becoming that other entirely; how an exchange may alter both sides without erasing the distance between them; how the living are neither strangers to the world nor masters standing outside it.

There shall be an age in which the names of living things fill towers. Their shapes shall be arranged in great books, their beginnings numbered, their endings recorded. The keepers of those books shall learn much, and what they learn shall matter. But when the last page is filled, a new seed shall open in a place no keeper has visited. Its form shall not refute the books. It shall reveal that the books have not exhausted the garden.

And an age shall come when the keepers of names are asked to name the first life. They shall search backward through the great branches of descent, past the earliest leaves, past the first mouths and the first eyes, past all that could have wondered what it was. At the place where their instruments grow uncertain, they shall find no throne, no single gate, no sentence proclaiming that before this there was nothing and after this there was life. There shall be a widening of possibility, a threshold whose stones are made of transitions. They shall call it a beginning because a beginning is easier to carry than a horizon.

Yet the horizon shall remain.

When the seventh light lies across the garden, the shadow of every tree shall point toward a place no tree can reach. There shall be a stillness there unlike rest, and a motion there unlike travel. Those who enter seeking the owner of the garden shall find no owner. Those who enter seeking the maker of its laws shall find no tablet. Those who enter seeking the first witness shall hear only the movement of leaves, and even that movement shall leave unanswered whether it was heard before they came.

In that hour the old question shall change its face. It will no longer ask whether existence remains when no living thing beholds it. It will ask why existence should ever have opened a place from which it could be beheld. The answer shall not descend as a voice. It shall not rise as a root. It shall appear, perhaps, in the distance between two things that cannot become one and cannot cease to belong to the same world.

The uncounted shall meet the counted. The enduring shall meet the passing. The separate shall meet without losing their difference. The named shall meet what no name can contain. Each meeting shall alter the garden, and none shall complete it.

There shall be a flower that opens only after the last gardener has forgotten its color. There shall be a path that exists because no one has walked it. There shall be a harvest that feeds no mouth yet changes the season of every orchard. Such things shall be called impossibilities until they are encountered, and then they shall be given smaller names. The smaller names shall be useful. They shall not be sufficient.

For what lives is not the entirety of what exists, and what exists is not exhausted by what lives. Magic shall pass through both, yet its passing shall not explain why either is here. The garden shall receive the question and return no doctrine. It shall receive the doubt and return no proof. It shall receive the certainty and grow around it until certainty is only one stone among innumerable stones.

At the farthest turning, where all paths appear to leave the garden, the first unplanted question shall be waiting. No gate shall guard it. No witness shall own it. The stars shall keep their distances. The waters shall descend. The mountains shall stand through changes no memory can gather. And somewhere, without proclamation, something living shall turn toward the world.

Whether that turning awakens the garden, or whether the garden has always been awake, shall remain unwritten in every language that follows.

The covenant shall endure without being signed.
